## What Does a Full-Stack Developer Actually Do?

A full-stack developer handles both the **front-end** (what users see and interact with) and the **back-end** (servers, databases, APIs, and business logic). Instead of needing a separate designer, developer, and backend team, a good full-stack developer can take your idea from concept to a live, functional product.

For an Australian business, this often means:

- Building a custom web application or internal tool
- Modernising an outdated website or legacy system
- Integrating payment gateways, booking systems, or third-party APIs
- Setting up databases and cloud infrastructure (AWS, Heroku, Railway)
- Creating a customer-facing portal or e-commerce store

## Why Hire an Australian Developer?

### 1. Real-Time Communication

No 12-hour time zone gaps. If something breaks on Friday afternoon before a long weekend, you can reach me. Working in the same time zone (AEST/AEDT) means faster iterations and clearer communication.

### 2. Understanding Australian Business Context

GST invoicing, Australian privacy law (Privacy Act 1988), WCAG accessibility compliance, and local payment gateways like POLi and BPAY are things I handle as standard. Many offshore developers simply are not familiar with the Australian regulatory and business context.

### 3. Face-to-Face When It Matters

For larger projects or ongoing retainers, in-person meetings are possible. Sometimes a whiteboard session saves hours of back-and-forth messages.

### 4. Supporting Australian Talent

Australia's tech ecosystem is growing, with strong developer communities in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, and beyond. Hiring locally keeps talent in the country and builds relationships that last.

## What Should You Look for in an Australian Web Developer?

Here is a practical checklist when evaluating any developer, local or remote:

**Portfolio and past work**
Ask to see real projects they have built. Screenshots are not enough. Look for live links or GitHub repositories you can review. Check whether their previous work matches the complexity of your project.

**Communication style**
A technically brilliant developer who cannot explain things to a non-technical stakeholder will frustrate you. In your first conversation, notice whether they ask clarifying questions or jump straight to "I can do that."

**Full-stack vs specialist**
For most small-to-medium business projects, a full-stack developer offers better value than hiring separate frontend and backend specialists. The tradeoff is depth: a full-stack developer may not be the world's best at any single thing, but they are good at everything you need.

**Tech stack alignment**
Ask what technologies they use. In 2026, the modern web development stack for most projects looks like: **React.js or Next.js** (frontend), **Node.js with Express** (backend), **MongoDB or PostgreSQL** (database), and **AWS or similar cloud** (hosting). **Client references or reviews**
Fiverr, Upwork, LinkedIn recommendations, or direct client testimonials are all valid. I have 50+ five-star reviews from global clients, and that track record means something.
